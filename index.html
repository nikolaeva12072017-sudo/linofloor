How to Choose Flooring for an Open Office Space

Open-plan offices are here to stay. But selecting the right flooring goes far beyond aesthetics. Acoustics, wear resistance, safety and maintenance costs -- each of these factors directly affects the comfort and productivity of your team.

Why open-plan spaces are a special case

Unlike private offices, open-plan environments place several simultaneous demands on flooring. People are in constant motion, office chairs roll continuously, and equipment is spread across the floor. The covering must withstand this day after day -- for years. At the same time, any hard surface amplifies reverberation, which means the material and texture of the floor have a direct impact on the acoustic comfort of the entire space.

Key selection criteria

  • 1
    Wear resistance. For high-traffic office environments, a minimum commercial use class of 33 is recommended. Pay particular attention to scratch and indentation resistance -- especially in areas with castor-wheeled chairs.
  • 2
    Acoustics. Hard, smooth surfaces reflect sound. Flooring with a soft or structured surface partially absorbs impact noise. Where noise is a concern, pairing the floor covering with an acoustic underlay is strongly advisable.
  • 3
    Moisture resistance. Areas adjacent to kitchens, entrance zones or restrooms require flooring with minimal water absorption. Seamless or minimal-joint coverings are considerably easier to keep hygienic.
  • 4
    Ease of maintenance. Open offices require daily cleaning. Non-porous surfaces do not accumulate dust and can be damp-mopped efficiently -- significantly reducing long-term cleaning costs.
  • 5
    Zoning flexibility. Open-plan layouts are frequently reconfigured. Modular floor coverings allow damaged sections to be replaced or zones to be redesigned without full removal of the floor.

Linoleum vs. vinyl tile: a comparison for office use

Both materials are widely used in commercial interiors and well suited to open-plan office conditions -- but each has distinct strengths.

Option 1
Commercial linoleum

Roll-format covering with no or minimal joints. Well suited to large, uniform areas. The softer base reduces impact noise and provides underfoot comfort during prolonged standing.

Seamless Better acoustics More complex installation
Option 2
LVT vinyl tile

Modular covering with extensive zoning and partial replacement possibilities. Moisture-resistant and easy to install. Ideal for spaces with complex configurations or frequent layout changes.

Modular Easy replacement Visible tile joints

Practical recommendations

Before specifying a floor covering, it is worth assessing the following: foot traffic intensity across different zones, the prevalence of castor-wheeled furniture, lighting levels (darker coverings conceal marks but can reduce the sense of space), and the long-term maintenance budget. In larger offices it is common practice to combine two types of covering -- linoleum in general circulation areas and vinyl tile in functional zones or high-load corridors.

Always confirm the commercial load class and relevant certifications with your supplier -- these factors have a significant impact on the actual service life of the covering.

Summary: The right flooring for an open-plan office is a balance between wear resistance, ease of maintenance and acoustic performance. Neither linoleum nor vinyl tile is a universal solution -- the optimal choice depends on the specific space, the budget and the pattern of use.

Maintenance

How to Care for Commercial Linoleum

Commercial linoleum is one of the most durable floor coverings available -- but its longevity depends directly on the quality of day-to-day maintenance. A consistent care routine not only preserves the appearance of the floor, but can extend its service life by decades.

Daily cleaning

For routine maintenance, dry sweeping or dust-mopping is sufficient to remove loose debris. Damp mopping with a pH-neutral cleaner diluted in clean water is recommended for daily or high-frequency cleaning. Avoid using excessive water -- linoleum should never be flooded, as moisture can penetrate seams and cause dimensional instability over time.

Periodic maintenance

Depending on traffic levels, periodic deep cleaning using a scrubbing machine with a mild cleaning agent is advisable. After deep cleaning, applying a maintenance polish or floor finish helps restore surface sheen and provides an additional protective layer against scuffs and wear.

What to avoid

  • --
    Abrasive scrubbing pads or steel wool -- these permanently damage the PUR surface layer
  • --
    Solvent-based or highly alkaline cleaning products -- they break down the binder and accelerate wear
  • --
    Prolonged standing water -- even water-resistant linoleum is not fully waterproof at the seams
  • --
    Rubber-backed mats left in place for long periods -- they can cause discolouration through chemical reaction

Summary: Consistent, gentle cleaning with appropriate products is the single most effective way to protect your linoleum investment. The floor covering itself is highly durable -- the care routine determines how long it stays looking its best.

Guide

How Long Does Commercial Flooring Last -- and What Affects It

The lifespan of a commercial floor covering is rarely determined by the material alone. Installation quality, subfloor condition, traffic intensity and maintenance practices all play an equally important role.

Typical service life by material

Commercial linoleum
20 to 40 years under standard commercial conditions. The natural linseed oil composition means the material continues to harden and stabilise over time.
LVT vinyl tile
15 to 25 years. Wear layer thickness is the primary factor -- a 0.7 mm wear layer is substantially more durable than a 0.3 mm alternative.

Key factors that affect longevity

  • 1
    Wear class specification. Using a Class 33 or 34 product in very heavy-traffic areas is the most important single decision. Under-specifying leads to premature wear regardless of maintenance quality.
  • 2
    Subfloor preparation. An uneven or damp subfloor causes adhesive failure, lifting and cracking. Proper levelling and moisture testing before installation are non-negotiable.
  • 3
    Maintenance consistency. Floors maintained correctly from day one retain their surface protection far longer. Deferred maintenance is the most common cause of early deterioration.
  • 4
    Furniture and equipment loads. Static loads from heavy shelving or machinery concentrate pressure. Felt pads and load-spreading plates prevent permanent indentation.

Summary: A well-specified, correctly installed and properly maintained commercial floor covering should comfortably reach or exceed its rated service life. The investment in quality at the specification stage pays dividends across the full lifecycle of the building.

Installation

How to Prepare a Subfloor Before Installation

Subfloor preparation is the foundation of a successful flooring installation. No matter how high the quality of the floor covering, a poorly prepared substrate will compromise the result -- causing lifting, cracking, visible irregularities and premature failure of the adhesive bond.

Step 1 -- Assessment

Begin with a thorough inspection of the existing subfloor. Check for cracks, soft spots, delamination and any signs of previous water damage. Any structural defects must be repaired before proceeding.

Step 2 -- Moisture testing

Moisture is the most common cause of adhesive failure. Conduct a calcium chloride test or use a relative humidity probe to verify that moisture levels are within the adhesive manufacturer's tolerances. For concrete subfloors, a reading below 75% RH is typically required.

Step 3 -- Levelling

The subfloor must be flat to within 3 mm over a 2-metre span for most resilient floor coverings. Apply a self-levelling compound to fill dips, low spots and surface irregularities. Allow full cure time before proceeding -- typically 24 hours minimum.

Step 4 -- Surface preparation

Remove all dust, grease, paint residues and adhesive from previous installations. A clean, dry and contaminant-free surface is essential for reliable adhesive bonding. Prime the subfloor if recommended by the adhesive manufacturer.

Summary: Subfloor preparation is time-consuming but non-negotiable. A properly prepared substrate is the single most important factor in ensuring a long-lasting, problem-free floor installation.

Comparison

Linoleum vs Vinyl Tile -- Which Is Right for Your Project?

Both linoleum and LVT vinyl tile are established commercial flooring solutions with strong performance credentials. Understanding their respective strengths is essential to making the right specification for a given project.

Linoleum
  • Natural, biodegradable composition
  • Service life up to 40 years
  • Inherently antibacterial surface
  • Superior acoustic performance
  • Seamless roll format available
  • Best for: healthcare, education, offices
LVT Vinyl tile
  • 100% waterproof construction
  • Authentic stone and wood aesthetics
  • Modular -- easy partial replacement
  • Compatible with underfloor heating
  • Wide design range
  • Best for: hotels, retail, residential

How to decide

Choose linoleum when natural composition, acoustic performance and long-term durability are the primary requirements -- particularly in healthcare, education or large office environments. Choose LVT when waterproofing, design flexibility or modular replacement are the deciding factors -- particularly in hospitality, retail and mixed-use developments.

In larger or more complex projects, combining both materials -- linoleum in general circulation areas and LVT in wet zones, feature areas or high-load corridors -- often delivers the best overall result.

Summary: There is no universal answer. The right choice depends on the specific functional requirements, the aesthetic brief and the long-term maintenance strategy of the project. Contact our team for a specification consultation.

How to Specify Flooring for a Large Commercial Project

Specifying flooring for a large commercial project involves considerably more than selecting a product. It requires a systematic approach to performance requirements, logistics, certifications and long-term costs -- all of which have a direct bearing on the success of the installation.

Define performance requirements by zone

Large commercial buildings typically have several distinct zones, each with different loading conditions. Entrance areas and main corridors require Class 34 (very heavy commercial). Office floors may be adequately served by Class 33. Back-of-house and service areas may have specific requirements for chemical resistance, ESD protection or hygiene standards. Map each zone before specifying a product.

Certifications to request

  • --
    EN ISO 10582 / EN 649 -- European classification for resilient floor coverings
  • --
    EN 13501-1 -- Fire reaction classification (Bf1-s1 or better for commercial use)
  • --
    ASTM D570-98 / VOC emission -- Formaldehyde-free and low-emission certification
  • --
    EN 13893 -- Slip resistance classification (DS class minimum for commercial spaces)

Logistics and lead times

For large-volume orders, confirm batch consistency with your supplier -- colour and texture variations between production batches are a common source of disputes on large projects. Request samples from the specific production batch if possible, and confirm delivery schedules against the installation programme well in advance.

Summary: Successful large-scale flooring specification requires early engagement with a knowledgeable supplier, a zone-by-zone performance analysis and rigorous documentation of certifications. Our team has extensive experience supporting architects, designers and project managers through the full specification process.
